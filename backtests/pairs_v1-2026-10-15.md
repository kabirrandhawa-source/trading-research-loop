# pairs_v1 (2026-10-15): FAILED

Hypothesis 11. Rules: stocks/pairs.py PARAMS_V1 (Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst distance method on the
point-in-time S&P 500). Prediction locked before the run: backtests/pairs_v1-prediction.md.
Raw numbers: backtests/pairs_v1-2026-10-15.json; trades: data/stocks/pairs_trades_v1.csv.
Development data only, 2017-01-03 to 2024-01-29 (85 months, ~500 candidates per formation).

## Result vs the pass bar
| | Result | Pass bar |
|---|---|---|
| Net annual return (committed capital) | **-0.27%** | > 0 |
| Monthly t-stat | **-0.29** | > 3 |
| Sharpe after costs | **-0.09** | > 1 |
| Positive full years (2017-2023) | **4 of 7** | 5 of 7 |
| Correlation with SPY | 0.01 | < 0.3 (passes) |

Gross before any costs: +0.54% a year, Sharpe 0.19, t 0.57. Costs (5 bps per leg, 0.5%/yr borrow)
take it below zero, but even gross there is nothing to find. Max drawdown -9.9% on committed capital.

## What happened
- 2,248 pair trades. 57% of trades won, but the losers are bigger: trades that converged averaged
  +5.1%, while the 58% that never converged and were closed at the 6-month end averaged -3.8%.
  The pairs that "break" (drift apart for good) eat the profits of the ones that snap back.
- The chosen pairs are mostly utilities (AEP, XEL, CMS, WEC, DTE...) and apartment REITs (AVB, EQR,
  UDR): the stocks that move least, so their gaps are small and rarely worth trading after costs.
- By year: 2017 -4.1%, 2018 +0.9%, 2019 -2.0%, 2020 +4.0%, 2021 -3.1%, 2022 +2.0%, 2023 +0.4%.

## Flaw found (does not change the verdict)
The share-class filter (daily return correlation > 0.98) missed News Corp NWS/NWSA, picked 45 times.
Those 63 trades made money (+0.39 in total on $1 legs); without them the result is slightly WORSE
(about -0.26% a year). A future version should exclude share classes by a company list, not correlation.

## Coke/Pepsi and Home Depot/Lowe's (descriptive only, not a test)
Neither was ever among the 20 closest pairs in any month. Traded by the same rules regardless, each
made money over these years (about +3% a year on committed capital, 58 and 53 trades). This is
hindsight: they were chosen because they're famous today, with only ~55 trades each. It is not
evidence of an edge, and testing hand-picked pairs would need its own hypothesis and prediction.

## Prediction check
1. Gross +1% to +4%/yr: wrong, lower (+0.5%).
2. Net -1% to +2%, Sharpe < 0.5, t < 2: right.
3. |corr with SPY| < 0.2: right (0.01). Losses clustered in 2020: wrong, 2020 was the best year.
4. Over 30% of trades fail to converge: right (58%).
5. KO/PEP, HD/LOW rarely picked and earn little: picked never (right); earned ~3%/yr standalone (wrong).

## Lesson
The textbook distance method on S&P 500 stocks has no edge 2017-2024, even before costs, matching the
literature's finding that it faded after the early 2000s. Not converging is the main risk, not costs.
